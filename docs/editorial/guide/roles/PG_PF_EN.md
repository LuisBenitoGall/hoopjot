# BRIDGE PURPOSE

The intersection between point guard and power forward appears when a player can help organize from very different places and actions.

They can initiate with the ball.

They can screen.

They can receive after the screen and make a second decision.

They can play from the high post.

They can connect through a handoff.

They can attack an advantage from outside or create one for someone else from inside.

This bridge does not create an intermediate identity or split the role 50/50.

The primary role pack always keeps the dominant perspective.

Only the nuances that come from combining organization, screening and the ability to connect advantages from different spaces are added here.

---

# P03 · YOUR FIRST MAJOR GOAL: BE RELIABLE

**Type:** BRIDGE

In this intersection, reliability includes understanding when the possession needs you to organize and when it needs you to facilitate someone else's action.

You can initiate.

You can screen for another creator.

You can receive afterwards and move the ball quickly.

You can occupy a space that gives a drive better options.

The combination works when you do not need to be either the beginning or the end of the action to influence it.

---

# P04 · DO NOT TRY TO LEARN EVERYTHING AT ONCE

**Type:** BRIDGE

Do not try to develop every perimeter creation tool and every interior solution at the same time.

Start by connecting simple actions:

- handle and pass safely;
- set a legal screen;
- roll into a useful space;
- catch and decide quickly;
- pass from the high post or short roll;
- re-enter the action after giving up the ball.

Then expand the reads and spaces from which you can create.

The combination gains value when its tools make one another better.

---

# P07 · REBOUNDING IS A DECISION

**Type:** BRIDGE

This intersection can connect the end of a defensive possession especially well with the beginning of the next one.

If you secure the rebound and a clear advantage exists, get your eyes up before automatically looking for a handoff.

An advance pass can start transition.

And if you can advance safely, you can also activate the first action without waiting for someone else to receive again.

If you do not secure the ball, recognize early whether you should provide an outlet or run to a useful position.

Rebounding and organizing are not separate actions when the next decision arrives immediately.

---

# P08 · RUN

**Type:** BRIDGE

In transition you can take on very different responsibilities.

Sometimes you will need to advance the ball.

Other times, run to the rim.

Arrive as the trailer.

Set an early screen.

Pop to maintain space.

Or receive in an intermediate area and connect the next action.

Do not decide your role out of habit.

Decide it based on the advantage that already exists.

The combination is especially useful when you can move from organization to depth, screening or secondary creation without slowing transition.

---

# P09 · UNDERSTAND SCREENS

**Type:** BRIDGE

This combination can create both before and after the screen.

It can begin as the ball handler or, after screening, become a short-roll receiver, passer, pop option, handoff hub or re-screener.

The nuance is recognizing that organization of the possession can change sides within the same action.

---

# P10 · TRAIN THE BORING FUNDAMENTALS

**Type:** BRIDGE

This intersection needs to connect fundamentals that are often trained separately.

Ball handling and passing.

Screening and rolling.

Catching and pivoting.

Short roll and passing.

Closeout attacks.

Footwork.

Finishing.

Open shooting when it is part of your game.

It is not enough to execute each action in isolation.

Work especially on the transitions between them:

**screen → catch → read;**

**handle → pass → re-enter the action;**

**interior catch → draw help → find another advantage.**

The ability to connect fundamentals matters more than collecting moves from two different positions.

---

# P11 · LEARN BASKETBALL OFF THE COURT

**Type:** BRIDGE

Pay special attention to where the possession is organized from.

Watch what happens after a screen, a short-roll catch, high-post catch or handoff, and how creation changes hands or space without losing continuity.

---

# P21 · MAKE IT EASY TO PLAY WITH YOU

**Type:** BRIDGE

This combination can make it especially easy to play with other creators.

You can organize one action without needing to organize the next.

You can set a screen and then become a passer.

You can receive inside and find an advantage outside.

You can occupy an interior space and leave it when a drive needs room.

You can deliver the ball through a handoff and keep playing.

The ability to create from several functions should reduce the team's dependency.

It should not create a new dependency on you.

---

# P22 · TALK

**Type:** BRIDGE

Alternating between actions near the ball and more interior positions can give you different views of the same possession.

When you are high on the floor, you can help organize.

When you are involved in a screen, you can communicate the action and what follows.

When you are closer to the rim, you can see help, cuts and rotations behind other players.

Learn to use the information provided by the place you occupy at each moment.

The combination expands your responsibility to communicate, not your right to talk more than anyone else.

---

# P26 · YOUR DEVELOPMENT PLAN

**Type:** BRIDGE

The hybrid emphasis is connecting on-ball creation with creation after screening.

Short roll, high post, handoff and interior passing should expand the contexts from which you can organize without becoming a second separate development plan.

---

# P27 · THE STATS THAT DO NOT SHOW UP

**Type:** BRIDGE

In this combination, notice especially:

- initiating an action and then becoming the screener;
- creating a second advantage from the short roll;
- connecting with the weak side from the high post;
- using a handoff without stopping the possession.

Ask yourself whether you created from several places without needing more of the ball.
