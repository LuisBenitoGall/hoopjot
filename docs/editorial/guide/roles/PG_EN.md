# ROLE PURPOSE

The point guard has a special responsibility for team organization.

That does not mean controlling every possession or monopolizing the ball.

It means helping the team reach good decisions earlier.

Key priorities include:

- ball security;
- organization;
- pace control;
- recognizing advantages;
- creating for others;
- passing;
- reading the pick-and-roll;
- drive-and-kick play;
- open shooting and pull-up shooting when appropriate;
- off-ball play after giving up the ball;
- on-ball defense;
- communication.

This role pack intervenes only in the zones authorized by `GUIDE_ROLE_MATRIX_EN.md`.

# P01 · YOUR STARTING POINT

**Type:** INSERT

For a point guard, the first responsibilities often appear very early.

Bring the ball up safely.

Help get the team into position.

Understand which action needs to begin.

Recognize when to run and when to organize.

Deliver the ball where it can create an advantage.

Defend with intensity against the player initiating the opponent's offense.

Communicate.

You do not need to master every form of creation immediately.

You need to make sure the team can start playing with clarity when you are on the court.

---

# P03 · YOUR FIRST MAJOR GOAL: BE RELIABLE

**Type:** INSERT

A reliable point guard is not simply someone who accumulates assists.

It is someone who helps the team function.

You can build that trust if you:

- get the ball into the frontcourt safely;
- know what the team wants to run;
- understand the pace the possession needs;
- avoid turnovers that do not come from trying to create a real advantage;
- quickly recognize where the advantage is;
- pass on time;
- defend the ball;
- communicate;
- keep the team organized when the game becomes chaotic.

The coaching staff should be able to trust that when the ball reaches your hands, the possession has a better chance of finding the right direction.

---

# P04 · DO NOT TRY TO LEARN EVERYTHING AT ONCE

**Type:** OVERRIDE

## LEVEL 1 · BE USEFUL

First learn to direct simple actions without losing control of the possession.

You should be able to:

- bring the ball up against reasonable pressure;
- maintain a secure dribble;
- pass accurately;
- initiate the systems;
- occupy the right spaces after passing;
- run in transition with good judgement;
- defend the ball;
- communicate;
- recognize where the next action should begin.

You do not need to create a spectacular advantage on every possession.

You need to allow the team to get into its offense.

## LEVEL 2 · CONTRIBUTE WITH CONTROL

Add the ability to read and use clear advantages.

Work especially on:

- changes of pace;
- ball protection;
- passing with both hands;
- drive-and-kick play;
- finishing when the defense gives up the rim;
- open shooting;
- basic pick-and-roll use;
- reading the first defensive help;
- turnover control.

Start deciding not only where the ball is, but where it should go next.

## LEVEL 3 · CREATE OR EXTEND ADVANTAGES

Then expand your solutions.

Learn to:

- manipulate pace;
- change angles;
- read different pick-and-roll coverages;
- attack a defensive recovery;
- find the roller;
- find the weak side;
- create off the dribble when the defense requires it;
- punish space with your shot;
- create a second advantage after the first one disappears.

Your goal is not to turn every possession into a display of creation.

It is to make more and more defenses choose among several bad answers.

---

# P06 · LEARN TO PLAY WITH YOUR BODY

**Type:** INSERT

For a point guard, using the body well has a lot to do with controlling space and pace.

Learn to:

- protect the dribble without unnecessarily separating from the ball;
- use changes of speed as well as changes of direction;
- stop in balance;
- put your body between the defense and the ball when appropriate;
- create passing angles;
- use your shoulder and hip legally to protect a path;
- finish near the rim without always depending on physically beating the defender;
- defend the ball without losing balance on every fake.

You do not need to be the fastest person on the court.

You need to learn when and how to use your speed differently.

---

# P07 · REBOUNDING IS A DECISION

**Type:** INSERT

Your rebounding responsibility does not disappear because you play point guard.

Box out your assigned player first.

Help finish the possession.

Long rebounds often come outside the lane and can allow you to start transition immediately.

But you also need to recognize when your team needs you to release early to provide the first passing option.

The right decision is not always to rebound and not always to run.

It is to know your responsibility on that possession.

---

# P08 · RUN

**Type:** INSERT

When your team wins the ball, one of your first jobs is to help decide whether there is a transition opportunity or whether the team should organize.

You can:

- provide an outlet;
- receive the first pass;
- advance the ball with a pass before a dribble;
- push the pace when there is an advantage;
- occupy a space that allows another player to handle;
- initiate the offense early if there is no clear transition advantage.

You do not need to touch the ball to direct every fast break.

You can also organize by occupying the right space.

When you lose the ball, your first responsibility is often close to the action.

Slow the transition.

Delay the advance.

Allow your defense to get organized again.

---

# P09 · UNDERSTAND SCREENS

**Type:** OVERRIDE

As a point guard, you should learn especially how to use the pick-and-roll to force the defense to make decisions.

Before using it, observe:

- the position of your defender;
- the position of the screener's defender;
- the available angle;
- the space around the action;
- the help defense;
- the weak side.

Set up your defender and arrive at the screen with an intention.

From there, change pace and read.

Does your defender go over or under?

Is there a switch?

Does the screener's defender contain, drop or step up aggressively on the ball?

Can you get to the rim?

Is the roll open?

Is there a short pass available?

Is the weak side open?

Is there a shot if the defense gives you space?

Also learn to:

- reject the screen when the defense anticipates it;
- change the entry angle;
- keep your dribble alive while you need more information;
- use a re-screen when the first action creates no advantage;
- attack a defensive recovery;
- come out ready to pass, shoot or drive.

The pick-and-roll is not about memorizing one answer.

It is about forcing a defensive decision and recognizing what the defense has conceded.

---

# P10 · TRAIN THE BORING FUNDAMENTALS

**Type:** OVERRIDE

Your priority fundamentals should allow you to play under pressure without losing your ability to read.

Work especially on:

- ball handling with both hands;
- dribble protection;
- changes of pace and direction;
- stops;
- pivots;
- one- and two-hand passing;
- passing with both hands;
- passing after penetration;
- finishing with both hands;
- body control near the rim;
- open shooting;
- pull-up shooting when it is part of your game;
- free throws;
- on-ball defensive footwork;
- stops, cuts and relocations after passing;
- catching ready to shoot, pass or attack again.

Do not turn ball handling into a collection of moves.

The goal is to reach useful spaces while keeping your head available to observe.

Do not turn passing into the thing you do only when you can no longer dribble either.

Learn to pass **earlier**, when the pass creates a greater advantage than keeping the ball.

And do not treat your involvement as finished after you give it up.

Relocate.

Provide a return passing lane.

Cut if the defense loses sight of you.

Remain a real threat so the next creator is not playing against a defense that can ignore you.

# P11 · LEARN BASKETBALL OFF THE COURT

**Type:** OVERRIDE

When you watch a point guard, do not look only at assists or how long they have the ball.

Ask yourself:

- When do they accelerate and when do they slow down?
- How do they get the team into the offense?
- Where do they look before receiving?
- When do they use the advance pass?
- How do they organize after transition produces no advantage?
- What do they read in the pick-and-roll?
- What do they do when the first advantage disappears?
- How do they move the defense before passing?
- When do they drive to score and when do they drive to create a pass?
- Where do they go after giving up the ball?
- How do they stay threatening without monopolizing the possession?
- How do they defend the player initiating the opponent's offense?
- What do they communicate?

Also notice how many good actions happen because they chose not to attempt something harder.

---

# P21 · MAKE IT EASY TO PLAY WITH YOU

**Type:** INSERT

As a point guard, you can make the game much easier for others.

Deliver the ball while the advantage still exists, not after it has disappeared.

Recognize who has a favorable matchup.

Help teammates receive in places where they can be useful.

Do not make the team wait while you decide every possession.

After passing, keep participating.

Space.

Cut.

Relocate.

Provide a return passing lane.

Screen when the action calls for it.

Being in charge of the ball does not mean the game always has to revolve around you.

---

# P22 · TALK

**Type:** INSERT

Your position allows you to communicate information before many actions begin.

You can help especially by:

- calling the system;
- organizing transition;
- identifying matchups;
- communicating the shot clock or game clock when relevant;
- calling screens;
- reminding teammates of a coverage;
- indicating where an action should begin;
- reorganizing the team after a chaotic possession.

Do not use communication to narrate the game constantly.

Use it to reduce uncertainty.

A good point guard helps several people understand earlier what is happening.

# P26 · YOUR DEVELOPMENT PLAN

**Type:** OVERRIDE

## PHASE 1 · RELIABILITY

First build a point guard who can be trusted.

Priorities:

- bring the ball up safely;
- know the systems;
- organize the starting positions;
- maintain a functional dribble under pressure;
- pass accurately;
- recognize simple advantages;
- reduce avoidable turnovers;
- defend the ball;
- communicate;
- control when to run and when to organize;
- continue occupying useful space after passing.

The goal is not yet to control the game.

It is to stop the game from controlling you.

## PHASE 2 · IMPACT

Start making the team better through your decisions.

Develop:

- changes of pace;
- drive-and-kick play;
- advance passing;
- pick-and-roll reads;
- creation for the roller;
- weak-side reads;
- shooting when the defense goes under or gives you space;
- finishing against help;
- the ability to create a second advantage;
- relocation and off-ball threat when a teammate creates;
- greater defensive influence on the opponent's organizer.

The goal is for your decisions to produce better possessions, not simply more actions for you.

## PHASE 3 · EXPAND YOUR GAME

Add tools that allow you to solve more complex defenses.

You can expand:

- reads against different coverages;
- manipulation of the help defender;
- off-the-dribble creation;
- shooting after a screen;
- more varied finishes;
- passes through smaller windows;
- off-ball play;
- the ability to alternate organization and scoring;
- the ability to function as a second threat when someone else initiates;
- pace control late in possessions and games.

Variety should make you harder to defend.

Not harder for your own team to understand.

---

# P27 · THE STATS THAT DO NOT SHOW UP

**Type:** OVERRIDE

To evaluate your game as a point guard, also notice actions such as:

- getting the team into the offense on time and in order;
- finding an advance pass that creates the first advantage;
- getting the ball to the right place before a window closes;
- drawing defensive help that allows someone else to score;
- creating a second advantage after the first drive;
- avoiding a turnover by keeping the possession alive;
- changing pace when the team needs it;
- organizing after a broken action;
- maintaining threat and good spacing after passing;
- defending the ball effectively;
- communicating a situation before it creates a mistake.

After the game, you can ask yourself:

- Did I help the team play with organization?
- Did I recognize when to speed up and when to slow down?
- Did I create advantages for others?
- Did I deliver the ball on time?
- Did I protect the possession well enough?
- Did I take good shots?
- Did I remain useful after passing?
- Did I defend the ball well?
- Did I communicate?
- Did I try to solve too much on my own?
