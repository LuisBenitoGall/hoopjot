# BRIDGE PURPOSE

The intersection between power forward and center appears when a player takes on important interior responsibilities and, at the same time, can connect actions from somewhat more open or intermediate spaces.

They can screen.

They can roll.

They can short roll.

They can play from the high post.

They can occupy spaces near the rim.

They can rebound.

They can protect the rim.

They can pass after drawing help.

They can pop when that threat is part of their game.

This bridge does not turn both positions into a 50/50 identity.

The primary role pack always keeps the dominant perspective.

Only the nuances that come from sharing interior responsibilities with different degrees of spacing and creation are added here.

---

# P03 · YOUR FIRST MAJOR GOAL: BE RELIABLE

**Type:** BRIDGE

In this intersection, reliability means sustaining interior responsibilities and being able to connect the possession when the action moves toward the high post, short roll or popping spaces.

Width adds value only if screening, rebounding, finishing and interior protection remain reliable.

---

# P04 · DO NOT TRY TO LEARN EVERYTHING AT ONCE

**Type:** BRIDGE

The shared progression should preserve a simple idea: first a reliable interior base; then solutions that allow you to connect from more spaces.

Short roll, high post, pop or attacking a closeout are useful expansions when they improve the reading of the same role, not when they replace responsibilities that are already established.

---

# P09 · UNDERSTAND SCREENS

**Type:** BRIDGE

This combination shares an important responsibility as a screener, but the second action can vary a great deal.

Roll, short roll, pop, re-screen, handoff, deep catch or weak-side pass allow you to alternate depth and spacing according to the defensive response.

The hybrid nuance is not turning every screen into the same roll.

---

# P10 · TRAIN THE BORING FUNDAMENTALS

**Type:** BRIDGE

In this combination, interior fundamentals remain the base.

Add short-roll work, high-post play, passing after help, open shooting or closeout attacks only to connect those responsibilities more effectively with intermediate or perimeter spaces.

Expansion should make your main actions harder to defend, not less reliable.

---

# P11 · LEARN BASKETBALL OFF THE COURT

**Type:** BRIDGE

Watch how interior presence is maintained without congesting the floor.

Notice what happens after screening, when roll, short roll or pop is used, how the high post is played and how rebounding and rim protection connect with the next action.

---

# P21 · MAKE IT EASY TO PLAY WITH YOU

**Type:** BRIDGE

This intersection contributes especially when interior presence also frees space.

Screening, rolling, providing a passing lane, occupying the high post or popping when appropriate should make the team's next decision easier.

You do not need to finish the action to have improved it.

---

# P26 · YOUR DEVELOPMENT PLAN

**Type:** BRIDGE

Development in this combination should expand solutions without weakening the interior base.

Short roll, high post, popping and passing add value when they are built on already reliable screening, rebounding, finishing and rim protection.

---

# P27 · THE STATS THAT DO NOT SHOW UP

**Type:** BRIDGE

In this combination, notice especially:

- alternating roll, short roll and pop according to the defense;
- maintaining interior presence without closing a driving lane;
- connecting from the high post without losing rebounding responsibility;
- protecting the rim and then taking part in the next action.

Ask yourself whether you expanded your solutions without weakening your interior impact.
