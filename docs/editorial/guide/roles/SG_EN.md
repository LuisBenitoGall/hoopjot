# ROLE PURPOSE

The shooting guard should be a threat even without the ball.

Their value does not depend only on how many shots they take.

It depends on how much space the defense has to respect, how they move, the quality of their catches and what they do once an initial advantage has already been created.

Key priorities include:

- foot preparation;
- catch-and-shoot play;
- off-ball movement;
- relocation;
- use of on-ball and off-ball screens;
- attacking closeouts;
- useful creation with one or two dribbles;
- passing after penetration;
- secondary playmaking;
- transition;
- perimeter defense;
- screen navigation;
- quick decisions.

A good shooting guard does not wait passively to receive.

They help create the conditions to receive with an advantage.

This role pack intervenes only in the zones authorized by `GUIDE_ROLE_MATRIX_EN.md`.

# P01 · YOUR STARTING POINT

**Type:** INSERT

For a shooting guard, one of the first responsibilities is learning to be useful without needing to dominate the ball.

Move with intention.

Occupy spaces the defense has to respect.

Prepare to receive.

Take the good shots the game gives you.

Attack a defense arriving off balance.

Move the ball when the advantage belongs to someone else.

Defend on the perimeter.

You do not need to prove immediately that you can create any shot.

You need to make sure the defense cannot forget about you.

---

# P03 · YOUR FIRST MAJOR GOAL: BE RELIABLE

**Type:** INSERT

A reliable shooting guard creates threat without disrupting how the team functions.

You can build that trust if you:

- arrive ready on catches;
- occupy corners, wings and spaces created off screens well;
- distinguish a good shot from a merely possible shot;
- maintain spacing;
- relocate after passing;
- attack closeouts with intention;
- use one or two dribbles to improve an advantage, not stop it;
- make the simple pass when help arrives;
- run in transition;
- defend perimeter players;
- navigate screens with effort and attention.

The coaching staff should be able to trust that you can create threat without needing every possession to start with you.

---

# P04 · DO NOT TRY TO LEARN EVERYTHING AT ONCE

**Type:** OVERRIDE

## LEVEL 1 · BE USEFUL

First build a simple, repeatable threat.

You should be able to:

- occupy perimeter spaces correctly;
- prepare your feet before receiving;
- catch ready to shoot, pass or attack;
- make open shots with stable mechanics;
- cut when the defense loses sight of both the ball and its matchup;
- relocate after passing;
- run wide in transition;
- defend off the ball;
- navigate simple screens;
- communicate.

You do not need to manufacture difficult shots.

You need to punish clear defensive mistakes.

## LEVEL 2 · CONTRIBUTE WITH CONTROL

Then learn to turn a first advantage into a second one.

Work especially on:

- coming off off-ball screens;
- catch-and-shoot;
- shooting after relocation;
- attacking closeouts;
- driving with one or two dribbles;
- finishing after an advantage;
- short pull-ups when the defense protects the rim;
- passing after penetration;
- reading the first help defender;
- secondary pick-and-roll creation when appropriate.

Start becoming dangerous not only when you are open, but also when the defense tries to close that space.

## LEVEL 3 · CREATE OR EXTEND ADVANTAGES

Then expand your answers.

Learn to:

- read different ways of defending off-ball screens;
- punish defenders who trail from behind;
- cut when the catch is denied;
- change direction and pace without the ball;
- attack a second rotation;
- create off the dribble when the first option disappears;
- use the pick-and-roll as a secondary creator;
- pull up when the defense concedes it;
- connect with the weak side;
- recreate threat after giving up the ball.

Your variety should make you harder to defend.

It should not turn every catch into an individual possession.

# P06 · LEARN TO PLAY WITH YOUR BODY

**Type:** INSERT

For a shooting guard, using the body well has a lot to do with preparing catches, changing pace and playing in balance at high speed.

Learn to:

- decelerate before receiving;
- arrive with your feet prepared;
- change pace to separate from your defender;
- use changes of direction off the ball;
- protect your running line when coming off a screen;
- catch while maintaining balance;
- attack closeouts without losing body control;
- absorb contact near the rim;
- stop to shoot or pass without needing extra steps;
- defend lateral movement and changes of direction without unnecessarily standing upright.

Your speed is more useful when you can accelerate and decelerate it.

Moving a lot is not useful if you arrive late, off balance or without knowing what you will do when you catch.

---

# P07 · REBOUNDING IS A DECISION

**Type:** INSERT

As a shooting guard, do not disappear from the possession when the shot goes up.

On defense, box out your matchup first and pay attention to long rebounds.

Many perimeter shots produce rebounds that come outside the lane.

You can help finish the possession and, if you secure the rebound, immediately start transition.

On offense, your responsibility depends on the team's balance.

Sometimes you should attack a long rebound.

Other times you need to protect defensive transition.

Do not confuse playing far from the rim with having no rebounding responsibility.

---

# P08 · RUN

**Type:** INSERT

A shooting guard can create an advantage before receiving the first pass.

In transition:

- run wide;
- occupy a perimeter lane early;
- arrive ready to shoot;
- attack a long closeout;
- cut to the rim if the defense abandons the interior too early;
- relocate if the first wave creates no advantage.

Do not run toward the ball out of habit.

Sometimes your best contribution is precisely to move away from it and stretch the defense.

When possession is lost, get back quickly.

Protect the perimeter.

Identify shooting threats.

Communicate matchups.

---

# P09 · UNDERSTAND SCREENS

**Type:** OVERRIDE

As a shooting guard, you should learn especially how to use off-ball screens and, when your role requires it, the pick-and-roll as a secondary creator.

Before coming off an off-ball screen, set up your defender.

Change pace and direction to force them to react before the screen.

Then read the defensive response.

If they trail, keep the advantage coming off the screen.

If they top-lock or anticipate, a cut to the rim may appear.

If they switch, recognize the new matchup quickly.

If they give space, arrive ready to catch and shoot.

If they aggressively deny the catch, continue the action instead of stopping.

Learn solutions such as:

- straight cut;
- curl;
- flare;
- backdoor cut;
- reject the screen;
- relocation;
- a second screen;
- catch and immediate attack.

When you use the pick-and-roll as a secondary creator, apply the same idea: provoke a response, read the coverage and decide quickly.

A screen is not only a way to get your own shot.

It is a way to move the defense and create an advantage for the team.

# P10 · TRAIN THE BORING FUNDAMENTALS

**Type:** OVERRIDE

Your priority fundamentals should allow you to turn space into threat before the defense can recover.

Work especially on:

- foot preparation before receiving;
- balanced stops;
- catching on balance and oriented to the next action;
- catch-and-shoot;
- shooting from corners and wings;
- shooting after relocation;
- shooting off screens;
- finishing with both hands;
- attacking closeouts;
- one or two functional dribbles;
- stopping after penetration;
- passing after penetration;
- the extra pass;
- enough ball handling to preserve an advantage;
- defensive footwork;
- screen navigation;
- changes of direction and pace without the ball.

Do not wait until the catch to start preparing the shot.

Your feet, balance and read should begin before the pass arrives.

And do not turn ball handling into an excuse to hold the ball.

A dangerous shooting guard may need very few dribbles to change a possession.

# P11 · LEARN BASKETBALL OFF THE COURT

**Type:** OVERRIDE

When you watch a shooting guard, do not look only at how many shots they make.

Ask yourself:

- Where do they position themselves when they do not have the ball?
- How do they prepare their feet before receiving?
- When do they stay spaced and when do they cut?
- How do they set up their defender before using a screen?
- Do they use the screener tightly?
- What do they do when the defense denies the catch?
- How do they relocate after passing?
- When do they attack a closeout?
- How many dribbles do they need to create an advantage?
- When do they shoot and when do they make the extra pass?
- How do they react to help after driving?
- How do they contribute in transition?
- How do they chase their matchup through screens?
- How do they remain a threat during a possession in which they barely touch the ball?

Pay special attention to what happens **before the catch**.

Many good shooting-guard actions are already half built when the ball reaches their hands.

---

# P21 · MAKE IT EASY TO PLAY WITH YOU

**Type:** INSERT

As a shooting guard, you can make the game easier for others by forcing the defense to respect your position.

Maintain space when a drive needs room.

Cut when your defender loses sight of you.

Relocate when help leaves your area.

Make the extra pass if a second player is even more open.

Attack the closeout to create something better, not simply to recover the shot that has already disappeared.

After driving, find the open teammate if the defense collapses.

Your threat has value even if you never receive.

If your movement makes help arrive late or prevents it from appearing, you are already affecting the possession.

---

# P22 · TALK

**Type:** INSERT

As a shooting guard, you need to communicate especially well in situations that happen away from the ball.

Call out:

- off-ball screens;
- defensive switches;
- cuts;
- shooters relocating;
- weak-side help;
- transition matchups;
- situations in which your defense needs to switch or recover.

On defense, navigating a screen is rarely one person's responsibility.

Communicate before you reach it.

An early call gives the defense time to decide whether to trail, switch, deny or adjust according to the team's coverage.

# P26 · YOUR DEVELOPMENT PLAN

**Type:** OVERRIDE

## PHASE 1 · RELIABILITY

First build a shooting guard who creates threat without demanding special possessions.

Priorities:

- occupy space well;
- prepare your feet;
- make open shots;
- relocate after passing;
- cut with intention;
- use simple screens;
- attack clear advantages;
- move the ball on time;
- run wide in transition;
- defend on the perimeter;
- navigate screens;
- communicate.

The goal is for the defense to have to pay attention to you even when the ball is not in your hands.

## PHASE 2 · IMPACT

Start turning defensive reactions into new advantages.

Develop:

- greater catch-and-shoot consistency;
- coming off off-ball screens;
- attacking closeouts;
- finishing after one or two dribbles;
- pull-ups when the defense protects the rim;
- passing after penetration;
- help reads;
- quicker relocations;
- secondary creation;
- defense against different perimeter players;
- better screen navigation.

The goal is to punish the first rotation without stopping the possession.

## PHASE 3 · EXPAND YOUR GAME

Add solutions that give the defense fewer ways to guard you.

You can expand:

- shooting after more complex movement;
- advanced off-ball screen reads;
- off-the-dribble creation;
- pick-and-roll as a secondary handler;
- passing after drawing two defenders;
- more varied finishes;
- pull-up shooting;
- the ability to alternate on-ball and off-ball play;
- defense against different perimeter profiles;
- creating advantages when the initial action breaks down.

Your development does not necessarily mean becoming the player who has the ball most.

It means being able to produce a good decision in more situations.

---

# P27 · THE STATS THAT DO NOT SHOW UP

**Type:** OVERRIDE

To evaluate your game as a shooting guard, also notice actions such as:

- maintaining spacing that opens a driving lane;
- forcing a defense to chase you away from the ball;
- using a screen in a way that creates an advantage even if you do not receive;
- relocating and creating a second pass;
- making the extra pass before the advantage closes;
- attacking a closeout correctly;
- creating a good action with one or two dribbles;
- defending a shooter without losing them through screens;
- communicating an action away from the ball;
- running wide and stretching transition;
- taking a good shot even if it does not go in;
- passing up a worse shot to keep a better advantage alive.

After the game, you can ask yourself:

- Was I a threat without the ball?
- Did I prepare my catches well?
- Did I take good shots?
- Did I attack closeouts well?
- Did I need too many dribbles to decide?
- Did I move the ball when the advantage was elsewhere?
- Did I use screens well?
- Did I maintain space after passing?
- Did I defend well off the ball?
- Did I navigate and communicate screens well?
