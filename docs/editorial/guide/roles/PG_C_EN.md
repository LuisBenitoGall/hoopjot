# BRIDGE PURPOSE

The intersection between point guard and center appears when the same function can help organize from very different spaces and actions.

They can initiate with the ball.

They can set a screen and then become the receiver.

They can play from the high post.

They can make decisions from the short roll.

They can connect through a handoff.

They can start transition after a rebound or outlet catch.

This bridge does not create an intermediate identity or split the role 50/50.

The primary role pack always keeps the dominant perspective.

Only the nuances that come from combining organization, interior play and the ability to connect advantages from different areas of the court are added here.

---

# P03 · YOUR FIRST MAJOR GOAL: BE RELIABLE

**Type:** BRIDGE

In this intersection, being reliable means being able to organize without always depending on the same place or action.

Sometimes the possession will need you to advance the ball.

Other times to set a screen.

To receive inside.

To pass from the high post.

To make a second decision after a short roll.

Reliability lies in recognizing which function helps the possession most and executing it without requiring everything to pass through your hands.

---

# P04 · DO NOT TRY TO LEARN EVERYTHING AT ONCE

**Type:** BRIDGE

Do not try to develop every perimeter playmaking tool and every interior solution at the same time.

Start by connecting simple actions:

- handle and pass safely;
- set legal screens;
- roll with intention;
- catch and protect;
- pivot and find a pass;
- make a quick decision from the short roll or high post;
- keep playing after giving up the ball.

Then expand the reads.

The combination gains value when you can organize from more than one space without losing simplicity.

---

# P07 · REBOUNDING IS A DECISION

**Type:** BRIDGE

This combination can turn a defensive rebound into the first offensive decision.

If you secure the ball, do not automatically look for a nearby handoff.

Get your eyes up.

An advance pass may be available.

A lateral outlet may be available.

You may be able to advance safely.

And it may be better to give the ball up early and run to another responsibility.

The ability to organize begins before crossing half court.

Sometimes it begins exactly when the rebound ends.

---

# P08 · RUN

**Type:** BRIDGE

In transition you can help from very different functions.

You can advance the ball.

Make the first pass and run to the rim.

Arrive to set an early screen.

Create depth.

Arrive at the high post.

Or become a secondary creation option after the defense has collapsed.

Do not decide in advance that you must handle or run inside.

Read where the advantage is.

The combination is useful precisely because it can help organize transition from different roles.

---

# P09 · UNDERSTAND SCREENS

**Type:** BRIDGE

This combination can organize the action both from the initial handle and from the catch after screening.

Short roll, high post, handoff, re-screen or a weak-side pass can turn the screener into a second source of creation.

The hybrid value lies in being able to organize the possession from both moments.

---

# P10 · TRAIN THE BORING FUNDAMENTALS

**Type:** BRIDGE

This intersection needs to connect perimeter and interior fundamentals without training them as two separate games.

Work especially on the connections between:

- handling and passing;
- catching and pivoting;
- screening and rolling;
- short roll and passing;
- rebounding and the first pass;
- high post and handoff;
- interior catches and help reads.

The transition between fundamentals matters as much as each fundamental in isolation.

**rebound → get your eyes up → pass;**

**screen → catch → decide;**

**handle → pass → occupy a new space;**

**catch inside → draw help → find another advantage.**

The combination loses value if every tool needs a different possession to exist.

---

# P11 · LEARN BASKETBALL OFF THE COURT

**Type:** BRIDGE

Watch how an organizing decision can appear before or after an interior action.

Notice the connection between rebound and first pass, screen and short roll, high post and handoff, and how an advantage is directed without needing to accumulate dribbles.

---

# P21 · MAKE IT EASY TO PLAY WITH YOU

**Type:** BRIDGE

This combination can simplify the game for others a great deal.

You can initiate a possession and then become the screener.

You can receive inside and return the ball to a better advantage.

You can create from the high post without holding the possession.

You can run a handoff and keep playing.

You can box out, make the first pass and run to another responsibility.

Your ability to organize from different places should help the team depend less on one person or one structure.

Not depend more on you.

---

# P22 · TALK

**Type:** BRIDGE

This intersection can communicate from very different perspectives.

When you are high on the floor, you can help organize the action.

When you screen, you can anticipate the coverage.

When you are near the rim, you can see cuts, help and rotations behind other players.

When the possession ends, you can help organize the outlet.

Learn to communicate according to the place you occupy at that moment.

Useful information changes with your position in the action.

---

# P26 · YOUR DEVELOPMENT PLAN

**Type:** BRIDGE

Development in this combination should expand the contexts from which you can organize: handling, rebounding, screening, short roll, high post and handoff.

The hybrid objective is to preserve simplicity and decision quality even when the place from which you connect the possession changes.

---

# P27 · THE STATS THAT DO NOT SHOW UP

**Type:** BRIDGE

In this combination, notice especially:

- turning a rebound into a good first pass;
- organizing after screening or receiving on the short roll;
- connecting a cut from the high post;
- creating an advantage through a handoff without holding the ball.

Ask yourself whether you helped organize from more than one context.
