# BRIDGE PURPOSE

The intersection between shooting guard and center appears when the same function can create threat both away from and near the rim.

They can space the floor.

They can move off the ball to receive.

They can set a screen.

They can roll to the rim.

They can occupy interior spaces.

They can receive inside and find a pass.

They can move back out and regain perimeter threat.

This bridge does not create an intermediate identity or split both positions 50/50.

The primary role pack always keeps the dominant perspective.

Only the nuances that come from combining perimeter threat, off-ball play, screening and interior presence are added here.

---

# P03 · YOUR FIRST MAJOR GOAL: BE RELIABLE

**Type:** BRIDGE

In this intersection, being reliable means your threat does not disappear when the space you occupy changes.

You can space the floor.

You can cut to the rim.

You can screen for someone else.

You can roll.

You can receive inside and move the ball quickly.

The combination gains value when the defense has to respect you in different areas without you turning every catch into an individual action.

---

# P04 · DO NOT TRY TO LEARN EVERYTHING AT ONCE

**Type:** BRIDGE

Do not try to develop every form of perimeter scoring and every interior solution at the same time.

First build a simple connection among:

- open shooting;
- off-ball movement;
- legal screening;
- rolling;
- interior catches;
- simple finishing;
- passing after help arrives;
- correct spacing.

Then expand the solutions.

The combination works when perimeter and interior threat reinforce each other.

Not when they look like two different games that never meet in the same possession.

---

# P07 · REBOUNDING IS A DECISION

**Type:** BRIDGE

This combination can face very different rebounding responsibilities depending on where it is when the shot goes up.

From outside, a long rebound or transition-balance responsibility may appear.

From an interior position, you may need to box out, attack the ball or keep an offensive rebound alive.

Do not decide according to your position label.

Decide according to:

- where you are;
- where your opponent is;
- who is protecting transition;
- what trajectory the shot may produce.

The combination's versatility also requires changing responsibility correctly when your location changes.

---

# P08 · RUN

**Type:** BRIDGE

In transition you can create threat in two very different ways.

You can run wide and stretch the defense.

Or you can run directly to the rim and create depth.

You can also move from one to the other within the same transition.

If the defense protects the rim too much, space may appear outside.

If it abandons the interior, a cut or deep catch may appear.

Do not automatically occupy the space that feels most familiar.

Occupy the one that makes the possession harder to defend.

---

# P09 · UNDERSTAND SCREENS

**Type:** BRIDGE

This intersection can transform the same action from the perimeter all the way to the rim.

It can use a screen to receive, set one to free someone else and then roll, receive inside, pop back out, offer a handoff or connect a pass.

The hybrid nuance is that the threat changes space without disappearing.

---

# P10 · TRAIN THE BORING FUNDAMENTALS

**Type:** BRIDGE

Connect perimeter-catching fundamentals with interior fundamentals.

Work especially on the transitions among:

- catch-and-shoot;
- cutting;
- screening;
- rolling;
- interior catches;
- pivots;
- finishing;
- passing after help arrives;
- perimeter relocation.

Do not train shooting and interior play as two separate identities.

For example:

**pop → catch → attack;**

**screen → roll → finish or pass;**

**catch inside → draw help → find outside;**

**cut → do not receive → create space again.**

The threat must survive the change of area.

---

# P11 · LEARN BASKETBALL OFF THE COURT

**Type:** BRIDGE

Watch how the player changes space without losing threat.

Notice the transition among spacing the floor, cutting, screening, receiving inside, passing against help and returning to a useful perimeter position.

Continuity between areas is the relevant trait.

---

# P21 · MAKE IT EASY TO PLAY WITH YOU

**Type:** BRIDGE

This combination can open a lot of space for others if it understands when to move toward the rim and when to move away from it.

Do not occupy inside the space a drive needs.

Do not stay outside if an action needs depth, a screen or a cut.

You can:

- space the floor;
- screen;
- cut;
- roll;
- receive inside;
- find a perimeter pass;
- relocate again.

Your usefulness increases when your movement gives others more space, not when you simply change areas.

---

# P26 · YOUR DEVELOPMENT PLAN

**Type:** BRIDGE

The hybrid emphasis is maintaining threat while moving between perimeter and interior spaces.

Shooting, cutting, screening, interior catching and passing should reinforce one another instead of developing as two independent games.

---

# P27 · THE STATS THAT DO NOT SHOW UP

**Type:** BRIDGE

In this combination, also notice:

- spacing the floor and then creating depth with a cut;
- screening and drawing interior help;
- receiving inside and finding a perimeter pass;
- popping back out after an action near the rim.

Ask yourself whether your threat remained alive when you changed areas.
