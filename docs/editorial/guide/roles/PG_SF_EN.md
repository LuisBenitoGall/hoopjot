# BRIDGE PURPOSE

The intersection between point guard and small forward appears when the same function can alternate between organizing a possession and connecting it from different spaces.

They can initiate with the ball.

They can receive after an initial advantage.

They can attack a closeout.

They can cut.

They can pass and move to another space.

They can help the possession continue without needing to get the ball back immediately.

This bridge does not split the identity between two positions.

The primary role pack always keeps the dominant perspective.

Only the nuances that come from combining organization and functional versatility are added here.

---

# P03 · YOUR FIRST MAJOR GOAL: BE RELIABLE

**Type:** BRIDGE

In this intersection, being reliable means being able to participate at different moments of the possession without losing clarity.

Sometimes you will need to initiate.

Other times you will connect an advantage through a pass, cut or short attack.

And sometimes your best contribution will be occupying a useful space and allowing someone else to keep creating.

Reliability lies in changing responsibility without turning every change into a new individual possession.

---

# P04 · DO NOT TRY TO LEARN EVERYTHING AT ONCE

**Type:** BRIDGE

You do not need to develop every creation tool of a point guard and every possible form of small-forward versatility at the same time.

First build a simple connection between both functions:

- handle safely;
- pass on time;
- catch ready;
- attack a clear advantage;
- cut when the space calls for it;
- keep participating after giving up the ball.

Then expand the situations from which you can create or connect an advantage.

The combination gains value from adaptability, not from the number of tools accumulated.

---

# P07 · REBOUNDING IS A DECISION

**Type:** BRIDGE

This combination can connect rebounding and transition organization in several ways.

If you secure a defensive rebound and have a clear outlet, an early pass can start the advantage immediately.

If you can advance safely, you can also launch transition without waiting for someone else to receive first.

And if you do not secure the ball, recognize early whether you should provide an outlet or run to a useful space.

The rebound does not end at the catch.

The next decision can determine the transition.

---

# P08 · RUN

**Type:** BRIDGE

In transition you can alternate between handling the first advantage and running to continue it.

If you receive early, get your eyes up before accumulating dribbles.

If someone else is already advancing the ball, you do not need to come toward it to get it back.

Run.

Create space.

Cut.

Become an option ahead of the ball.

The versatility of this combination appears when you can help organize transition both with and without the ball.

---

# P09 · UNDERSTAND SCREENS

**Type:** BRIDGE

This intersection can take part in the action as a ball handler, an off-ball user or a screener.

Its value appears when it understands which role it occupies at each moment and can connect the first screen to a second action: receive, cut, pop, create again or screen for someone else.

---

# P10 · TRAIN THE BORING FUNDAMENTALS

**Type:** BRIDGE

Connect organization fundamentals with continuity fundamentals.

You need to be able to:

- handle and pass under reasonable pressure;
- catch ready;
- attack a closeout without needing too many dribbles;
- stop and pass again in balance;
- cut and relocate;
- finish a simple advantage;
- take over handling again if the possession requires it.

Do not train these tools as separate compartments.

The value appears when you move from one to another without the possession losing rhythm.

---

# P11 · LEARN BASKETBALL OFF THE COURT

**Type:** BRIDGE

Watch how responsibility changes within the same possession.

Notice when the player initiates, when they connect an advantage through a pass or closeout, and when they cut or maintain spacing after giving up the ball.

The hybrid key is re-entering the action without stopping the offense.

---

# P21 · MAKE IT EASY TO PLAY WITH YOU

**Type:** BRIDGE

This combination can make it especially easy to share the court with other creators.

If someone else has an advantage, you do not need to get the ball back to participate.

You can maintain spacing.

Cut.

Screen.

Relocate.

Make the pass that connects the next action.

And if the possession needs organization again, you can take it over.

Versatility has value when it reduces dependencies, not when it increases the need to touch the ball.

---

# P22 · TALK

**Type:** BRIDGE

Alternating functions also expands the information you can provide.

In some actions you will be close to the ball and can help organize.

In others you will have a better view of cuts, help, switches or weak-side actions.

Learn to communicate from the place you occupy at that moment.

Not from the position written next to your name.

---

# P26 · YOUR DEVELOPMENT PLAN

**Type:** BRIDGE

Across your development, maintain good decisions even as the place and responsibility from which you participate change.

The combination improves when organization, passing, cutting, closeout attacks and off-ball play form one continuous game.

---

# P27 · THE STATS THAT DO NOT SHOW UP

**Type:** BRIDGE

In this combination, also notice:

- initiating and then occupying a useful space;
- connecting two advantages with a simple pass;
- attacking a closeout without stopping the action;
- taking over organization when a possession breaks down.

Ask yourself whether your versatility simplified the possession.
