# BRIDGE PURPOSE

The intersection between shooting guard and power forward appears when a perimeter threat can also participate as a screener and connector from intermediate spaces.

They can space the floor.

They can use an off-ball screen.

They can set a screen for someone else.

They can pop afterwards.

They can roll.

They can receive on the short roll.

They can attack a closeout.

They can connect an advantage through a pass.

This bridge does not blend both positions 50/50.

The primary role pack always keeps the dominant perspective.

Only the nuances that come from combining perimeter threat, screening and the ability to continue actions from different spaces are added here.

---

# P03 · YOUR FIRST MAJOR GOAL: BE RELIABLE

**Type:** BRIDGE

In this intersection, being reliable means creating threat without depending on only one way of participating.

You can create space with your position.

You can catch ready to shoot.

You can set a useful screen.

You can roll or pop.

You can attack a short advantage.

You can move the ball when help arrives.

The combination gains value when the team can use you as a threat before, during and after a screening action.

---

# P04 · DO NOT TRY TO LEARN EVERYTHING AT ONCE

**Type:** BRIDGE

You do not need to develop every form of perimeter scoring and every solution of a mobile interior player at the same time.

First build a reliable connection among:

- open shooting;
- off-ball movement;
- legal screening;
- rolling;
- popping;
- attacking closeouts;
- a simple pass after an advantage.

Then expand the reads.

The combination works when each new tool improves the previous ones.

Not when it simply accumulates tools from two different positions.

---

# P06 · LEARN TO PLAY WITH YOUR BODY

**Type:** BRIDGE

This combination often requires switching between perimeter movement and screening contact.

Learn to arrive balanced in both contexts.

Moving to receive requires rhythm and control.

Screening requires a base, an angle and legal position.

Attacking a closeout requires deceleration and ball protection.

Rolling after screening requires accelerating again.

The value lies in changing tasks without losing balance or clarity.

---

# P08 · RUN

**Type:** BRIDGE

In transition you can create space in several ways.

Sometimes by running wide.

Other times by arriving as the trailer.

Or by arriving ready to set an early screen and pop afterwards.

Do not automatically occupy a perimeter lane or always run to the rim.

Read which space is missing.

The combination is useful when it can provide width or join a second action without slowing transition.

---

# P09 · UNDERSTAND SCREENS

**Type:** BRIDGE

This combination can alternate between using a screen as a perimeter threat and setting one to become a different threat afterwards.

After screening, it can pop, roll, short roll, relocate, re-screen or offer a handoff.

The defense has to choose between protecting the pop, the roll and the next action.

---

# P10 · TRAIN THE BORING FUNDAMENTALS

**Type:** BRIDGE

Connect perimeter-threat fundamentals with screening and rolling fundamentals.

Work especially on:

- foot preparation;
- catch-and-shoot;
- attacking closeouts;
- passing after penetration;
- screen positioning;
- roll;
- pop;
- short roll;
- catching in intermediate spaces;
- one or two functional dribbles;
- passing after help arrives;
- finishing;
- rebounding;
- relocation.

Do not train shooting and screening as two separate identities.

The defense should have problems precisely because the same action can end in a pop, roll or pass.

---

# P11 · LEARN BASKETBALL OFF THE COURT

**Type:** BRIDGE

Pay special attention to what happens around screens.

Watch when perimeter threat is maintained, when the player becomes the screener, and how they decide afterwards among pop, roll, short roll, pass or second action.

---

# P21 · MAKE IT EASY TO PLAY WITH YOU

**Type:** BRIDGE

This combination is especially easy to play with when your perimeter threat also helps create advantages for others.

Space the floor.

Screen.

Roll.

Pop when it makes sense.

Catch and move the ball.

Attack late help.

Box out.

You do not need to choose between “being a shooter” and “being a screener.”

The value lies in allowing both responsibilities to coexist without stopping the possession.

---

# P22 · TALK

**Type:** BRIDGE

This intersection can participate both in perimeter actions and screening coverages.

Communicate from both.

Call off-ball screens and relocations when you are outside.

And when you take part in pick-and-roll actions, communicate coverage, switch, help and recovery.

The combination expands the contexts in which you need to share information.

It does not change the principle: useful, clear and timely information.

---

# P26 · YOUR DEVELOPMENT PLAN

**Type:** BRIDGE

Hybrid development should connect perimeter threat with play after screening.

The combination improves when shooting, popping, rolling, short roll and passing become related answers to the same defensive reaction.

---

# P27 · THE STATS THAT DO NOT SHOW UP

**Type:** BRIDGE

In this combination, notice especially:

- setting a screen and remaining a threat by popping;
- rolling and freeing a shot for someone else;
- making a good decision from the short roll;
- moving a help defender because of your perimeter threat.

Ask yourself whether you alternated pop and roll well.
