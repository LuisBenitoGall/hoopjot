# ROLE PURPOSE

The center often has a special responsibility for the areas closest to the rim.

That does not mean staying in the paint or limiting the game to post actions.

It means many possessions depend on how you:

- occupy space;
- screen;
- roll;
- finish;
- box out;
- protect the rim;
- help;
- communicate;
- pass from interior positions;
- create depth so others can play.

Key priorities include:

- defensive rebounding;
- offensive rebounding;
- rim protection;
- screening;
- rolling;
- short roll when appropriate;
- finishing;
- seals and interior catches;
- interior spacing;
- passing from the post and interior areas;
- pick-and-roll defense;
- help defense;
- defensive communication.

A good center can change a possession many times without receiving the ball.

This role pack intervenes only in the zones authorized by `GUIDE_ROLE_MATRIX_EN.md`.

# P01 · YOUR STARTING POINT

**Type:** INSERT

For a center, the first responsibilities often appear in actions that repeat constantly.

Run.

Protect the rim.

Box out.

Attack the ball.

Set legal, useful screens.

Roll with intention.

Occupy spaces that allow others to drive.

Finish simple advantages.

Pass when help arrives.

Communicate.

You do not need to prove immediately that you have a complete collection of post moves or perimeter skills.

You need to make the team trust you in possessions that are decided near the rim.

---

# P03 · YOUR FIRST MAJOR GOAL: BE RELIABLE

**Type:** INSERT

A reliable center gives stability on both ends of the court.

You can build that trust if you:

- protect the rim without chasing every action;
- box out before looking for the ball;
- set screens with good angles;
- roll and create depth;
- occupy the dunker spot, low post, high post or screening spaces correctly according to the action;
- finish simple advantages;
- protect the ball after receiving;
- make the simple pass when help arrives;
- defend the pick-and-roll according to the coverage;
- communicate what is happening in front of you;
- stay active after the first effort.

The coaching staff should be able to trust that you help finish defensive possessions and give structure to offensive ones.

---

# P04 · DO NOT TRY TO LEARN EVERYTHING AT ONCE

**Type:** OVERRIDE

## LEVEL 1 · BE USEFUL

First build a center who is reliable in the basic actions of the role.

You should be able to:

- run to the rim;
- occupy interior spaces correctly;
- set legal screens;
- roll after screening;
- box out;
- attack the ball;
- finish near the rim;
- catch and protect;
- make simple passes;
- provide defensive help;
- protect the rim;
- communicate.

You do not need to demand the ball constantly.

You need to create value in actions that happen even when you do not receive it.

## LEVEL 2 · CONTRIBUTE WITH CONTROL

Then add tools to solve the advantages that reach you.

Work especially on:

- finishing with both hands;
- deep catches;
- seals;
- pivots;
- finishing through contact;
- roll;
- short roll when it appears;
- passing from the low or high post;
- reading help defense;
- offensive rebounding;
- second finishes;
- different pick-and-roll defensive coverages.

Start turning every interior catch into a quick, controlled decision.

## LEVEL 3 · CREATE OR EXTEND ADVANTAGES

Then expand your solutions.

Learn to:

- pass after drawing help;
- play from the short roll;
- connect cuts from the high post;
- use handoffs;
- re-screen;
- punish defensive switches;
- gain position without the ball;
- finish from different angles;
- make an occasional decision off the dribble when the situation requires it;
- pop or shoot if that threat is genuinely part of your game;
- defend increasingly complex actions without losing interior protection.

Your development is not about moving progressively farther from the rim.

It is about adding solutions without losing the responsibilities that support your role.

# P06 · LEARN TO PLAY WITH YOUR BODY

**Type:** INSERT

For a center, using the body well has a lot to do with occupying, protecting and recovering space.

Learn to:

- establish a stable base when screening;
- seal without using your arms illegally;
- gain position before receiving;
- protect the ball;
- maintain balance through contact;
- use pivots to create angles;
- box out with your body before looking for the ball;
- maintain verticality near the rim;
- contain a drive without jumping too early;
- change direction after a screen;
- recover defensive position after helping.

You do not need to be the tallest or strongest person on the court to learn these principles.

Your body does not automatically determine your effectiveness.

Position, balance, timing and angles matter just as much as physical qualities.

---

# P07 · REBOUNDING IS A DECISION

**Type:** INSERT

For a center, ending the defensive possession with the rebound is often a central responsibility.

Do not look at the ball first.

When the shot goes up:

- identify who you need to box out;
- gain position;
- create legal contact;
- keep your opponent out;
- then attack the ball decisively.

Not every rebound you help secure will appear under your name.

If you correctly box out the main threat and a teammate gets the ball, you have fulfilled an essential part of the possession.

On offense, do not chase every shot without judgement.

Read:

- where the shot comes from;
- where you are;
- where your defender is;
- what trajectory may result;
- who is protecting transition balance.

Look for second chances when your position gives you a real advantage.

Rebounding is not a competition to jump more often.

It is a competition to arrive in better position.

---

# P08 · RUN

**Type:** INSERT

A center who runs well forces the defense to protect the rim from the first seconds.

In transition you can:

- run directly to the rim;
- gain deep position;
- create a passing lane;
- drag a defender with you and open space behind;
- set an early screen;
- arrive ready for a second action;
- occupy the dunker spot if transition flows into half-court offense.

Do not run only to receive.

Your run can force the defense to collapse and free perimeter space even if the ball never reaches you.

If you secure the defensive rebound, look for a clear outlet first.

A good first pass usually advances the ball faster than several dribbles.

When possession is lost, recover with urgency.

Protect the rim and the area of greatest danger when that is your responsibility.

Then organize matchups and communicate.

---

# P09 · UNDERSTAND SCREENS

**Type:** OVERRIDE

As a center, screening can be one of your most frequent ways to create advantages for the team.

Do not measure the action only by the initial contact.

After the screen, you can:

- roll hard to the rim;
- make a short roll;
- re-screen;
- change the angle;
- gain deep position;
- offer a handoff;
- occupy a space that opens a driving lane;
- pop when that threat is part of your game.

Read the defense.

If two defenders attack the ball, look for the space they leave behind.

If they switch, try to use the new matchup without stopping the entire possession.

If the defender drops too far, another advantage may appear.

If the first screen creates nothing, a new angle or second action may be more useful than standing still.

A good center evaluates screens by the advantages they help create and by the quality of the next decision.

# P10 · TRAIN THE BORING FUNDAMENTALS

**Type:** OVERRIDE

Your priority fundamentals should allow you to gain and keep useful positions near the rim.

Work especially on:

- screen angles and positioning;
- seals;
- interior catches;
- ball protection;
- pivots;
- footwork;
- finishing with both hands;
- using the backboard;
- finishing from different angles;
- finishing through contact;
- passing from the low post;
- passing from the high post;
- passing after help arrives;
- short roll;
- rebounding;
- box outs;
- second finishes;
- defensive verticality;
- short help-and-recovery movements;
- pick-and-roll defense across different coverages.

You do not need ten post moves.

You need a few fundamentals you can execute with balance, reading and speed.

And do not turn every interior catch into an obligation to shoot.

Sometimes the center's best action is to catch, draw help and immediately find someone else.

# P11 · LEARN BASKETBALL OFF THE COURT

**Type:** OVERRIDE

When you watch a center, do not follow only dunks, blocks or rebounds.

Ask yourself:

- Where do they position themselves before the shot?
- Who do they box out when the rebound begins?
- How do they protect the rim without unnecessarily abandoning position?
- What angle do they use when screening?
- What do they do immediately after the screen?
- When do they roll and when do they short roll?
- How do they create depth without receiving?
- Where do they position themselves during a drive?
- How do they seal before asking for the ball?
- What do they do when defensive help arrives?
- How do they pass from the high or low post?
- What pick-and-roll coverage do they use?
- What do they communicate to the on-ball defender?
- How do they recover after helping?
- How many possessions do they change without touching the ball?

Pay special attention to what they do **before the ball arrives**.

Interior position is often won before the catch, not after it.

---

# P21 · MAKE IT EASY TO PLAY WITH YOU

**Type:** INSERT

As a center, you can make the game easier for the whole team without asking for a single possession for yourself.

Set good screens.

Create depth by running to the rim.

Do not close a driving lane by occupying space someone else needs.

Provide a passing lane when the defense helps.

Pass quickly when you draw two defenders.

Box out so a teammate can secure the rebound.

Protect the rim.

Help.

Communicate.

Re-screen if the first action creates no advantage.

A useful center does not need to finish every possession near the rim.

They need to make that area easier for their team to play in and harder for the opponent.

---

# P22 · TALK

**Type:** INSERT

From interior positions, you can see many actions developing in front of you.

Use that information.

Communicate especially:

- pick-and-roll screens;
- pick-and-roll coverage;
- cuts;
- help;
- switches;
- recoveries;
- players occupying the dunker spot;
- threats near the rim;
- rebounding;
- transition matchups.

If you are the last line of defense, your communication should begin before the problem exists.

Call the screen.

Call the coverage.

Communicate the help.

Then identify who needs to recover.

Rim protection does not begin when someone jumps to block a shot.

It begins much earlier, with position and information.

# P26 · YOUR DEVELOPMENT PLAN

**Type:** OVERRIDE

## PHASE 1 · RELIABILITY

First build a center who can be trusted to protect and finish possessions.

Priorities:

- run to the rim;
- occupy interior spaces correctly;
- set legal, useful screens;
- roll with intention;
- box out;
- attack the ball;
- finish simple advantages;
- protect the ball;
- make basic passes;
- provide defensive help;
- protect the rim;
- communicate.

The goal is for your presence to provide security near the rim on both ends of the court.

## PHASE 2 · IMPACT

Start turning those responsibilities into bigger advantages.

Develop:

- better screening angles;
- roll;
- short roll;
- seals;
- finishing through contact;
- offensive rebounding;
- passing from the post;
- passing after help arrives;
- reading defensive switches;
- better pick-and-roll defense;
- more precise help;
- recovery after helping;
- rim protection without unnecessary fouls.

The goal is for your influence to be felt in more possessions, even when you do not finish the action.

## PHASE 3 · EXPAND YOUR GAME

Add tools that allow the team to use you in more ways without losing your identity.

You can expand:

- advanced passing from the short roll;
- high-post reads;
- handoffs;
- play against defensive switches;
- more varied finishes;
- brief creation after the catch;
- open shooting if it is genuinely part of your development;
- popping after a screen when it makes sense;
- defense across different coverages;
- the ability to participate in actions farther from the rim;
- connections among screening, passing and second actions.

Expansion should increase your solutions.

It should not make you abandon what already makes you a good center.

---

# P27 · THE STATS THAT DO NOT SHOW UP

**Type:** OVERRIDE

To evaluate your game as a center, also notice actions such as:

- boxing out an opponent so someone else can secure the rebound;
- altering a drive without needing to block it;
- preventing a drive simply through your position;
- setting a screen that creates an advantage;
- re-screening with a better angle;
- running to the rim and forcing the defense to collapse;
- sealing and opening a driving lane for another player;
- making a short roll that forces a rotation;
- finding a pass after drawing help;
- protecting the rim without fouling;
- helping and recovering;
- communicating a coverage before the screen;
- keeping an offensive rebound alive;
- creating a second-chance opportunity;
- occupying the right space during a drive.

After the game, you can ask yourself:

- Did I finish defensive possessions?
- Did I box out before looking for the rebound?
- Did I protect the rim with good position?
- Did I commit avoidable fouls trying to block everything?
- Did I set screens that created advantages?
- Did I do something useful after screening?
- Did I run with intention?
- Did I occupy interior spaces well?
- Did I pass when help arrived?
- Did I create second-chance opportunities?
- Did I communicate coverages?
