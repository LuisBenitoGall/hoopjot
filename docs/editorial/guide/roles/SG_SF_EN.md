# BRIDGE PURPOSE

The intersection between shooting guard and small forward appears when perimeter threat coexists with broader participation in the possession.

They can create space through shooting and off-ball movement.

They can attack a closeout.

They can cut.

They can connect an advantage through a pass.

They can help on the boards.

They can defend different perimeter actions.

This bridge does not create a third identity or split both positions 50/50.

The primary role pack always keeps the dominant perspective.

Only the nuances that come from combining perimeter threat and functional versatility are added here.

---

# P03 · YOUR FIRST MAJOR GOAL: BE RELIABLE

**Type:** BRIDGE

In this intersection, being reliable means creating threat without being limited to one way of participating.

You can be useful by shooting.

Also by cutting.

Attacking an advantage.

Making the extra pass.

Helping on the boards.

Defending on and off the ball.

The combination gains value when the defense has to respect your perimeter threat but the team can still rely on you when no shot appears.

---

# P04 · DO NOT TRY TO LEARN EVERYTHING AT ONCE

**Type:** BRIDGE

You do not need to develop every possible scoring method and all of a small forward's versatility at the same time.

First build a common base:

- catch ready;
- make open shots;
- maintain spacing;
- attack clear closeouts;
- cut with intention;
- move the ball;
- defend;
- help finish the rebound.

Then add solutions when the defense takes away your first option.

The combination works best when you expand your game around a reliable threat, not when you try to turn every tool into a specialty.

---

# P07 · REBOUNDING IS A DECISION

**Type:** BRIDGE

This combination can have an especially useful responsibility for rebounds that come toward the perimeter.

Do not automatically leave the possession because the shot was taken far from you.

Box out first.

Read the trajectory.

Attack a long rebound when you have the advantage.

And recognize when your responsibility is protecting defensive transition.

Perimeter threat and rebounding are not incompatible functions.

The decision depends on where you are when the shot goes up.

---

# P09 · UNDERSTAND SCREENS

**Type:** BRIDGE

This intersection should be especially comfortable connecting off-ball screens with second actions.

Coming off a screen can lead to a shot, curl, cut, immediate attack, extra pass, relocation or even a later screen for someone else.

The hybrid advantage is that off-ball movement does not necessarily end with your own catch.

---

# P10 · TRAIN THE BORING FUNDAMENTALS

**Type:** BRIDGE

Connect perimeter threat with fundamentals that let you keep playing after the first catch.

Work especially on:

- foot preparation;
- catch-and-shoot;
- oriented catches;
- attacking closeouts;
- one or two functional dribbles;
- the extra pass;
- passing after penetration;
- cuts;
- relocations;
- finishing;
- rebounding from perimeter positions;
- defense on and off the ball.

The goal is not to add dribbling for the sake of dribbling.

It is to make sure a defense that closes your shot has not also ended your usefulness.

---

# P11 · LEARN BASKETBALL OFF THE COURT

**Type:** BRIDGE

When watching this combination, look at what happens when the first perimeter option disappears.

Notice how a denied catch or closed shot becomes a cut, closeout attack, extra pass, relocation, defense or rebounding contribution on the next action.

---

# P21 · MAKE IT EASY TO PLAY WITH YOU

**Type:** BRIDGE

This combination makes the game easier when perimeter threat does not require stopping the possession.

Maintain space.

Cut when appropriate.

Make the extra pass.

Attack a disorganized recovery.

Help on the boards.

Defend the next action.

If an advantage created by your movement ends up benefiting someone else, the action still has value.

Threat is a tool for the team, not an obligation for you to finish the possession.

---

# P26 · YOUR DEVELOPMENT PLAN

**Type:** BRIDGE

The emphasis of this combination is maintaining perimeter threat even when the first option disappears.

Shooting, closeout attacks, cutting, passing and defense should expand your usefulness around a reliable base, not compete to become separate specialties.

---

# P27 · THE STATS THAT DO NOT SHOW UP

**Type:** BRIDGE

In this combination, also notice:

- remaining a threat even when no shot appears;
- preserving an advantage after a closeout;
- creating space through movement without receiving;
- continuing to contribute through passing, defense or rebounding after the first action.

Ask yourself whether your game stayed alive when your initial option disappeared.
