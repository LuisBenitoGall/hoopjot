# BRIDGE PURPOSE

The intersection between small forward and center appears when a player can alternate between connecting the game from perimeter spaces and taking on responsibilities near the rim.

They can space the floor.

They can cut.

They can attack a closeout.

They can set a screen.

They can roll.

They can occupy interior spaces.

They can rebound.

They can pass from inside.

They can help protect the rim.

This bridge does not create an intermediate identity or split both positions 50/50.

The primary role pack always keeps the dominant perspective.

Only the nuances that come from combining perimeter versatility with interior responsibilities are added here.

---

# P03 · YOUR FIRST MAJOR GOAL: BE RELIABLE

**Type:** BRIDGE

In this intersection, being reliable means being able to contribute when the possession takes you from the perimeter to areas near the rim and back again.

You can maintain spacing.

Cut.

Screen.

Roll.

Box out.

Finish a simple advantage.

Make a pass from inside.

Help defensively near the rim.

The combination gains value when your usefulness does not disappear as you change areas.

---

# P04 · DO NOT TRY TO LEARN EVERYTHING AT ONCE

**Type:** BRIDGE

You do not need to develop every small-forward tool and every center solution at the same time.

First build a base that works in both contexts:

- occupy space correctly;
- cut with intention;
- catch ready;
- finish simple advantages;
- set legal screens;
- roll afterwards;
- pass when help arrives;
- box out;
- provide defensive help;
- protect the rim when appropriate.

Then expand the contexts from which you can contribute.

The combination works when you can change responsibility without losing reliability.

---

# P07 · REBOUNDING IS A DECISION

**Type:** BRIDGE

This combination can reach the rebound from very different places.

If you start outside, pay attention to long rebounds and weak-side crashes.

If you are already near the rim, boxing out first may matter more than immediately chasing the ball.

Do not decide according to the position written next to your name.

Decide according to where you are when the shot goes up.

On offense, also recognize when you can attack a second chance and when you need to protect team balance.

Changing areas means changing responsibility correctly.

---

# P08 · RUN

**Type:** BRIDGE

In transition you can create advantage by providing width or depth.

You can run a perimeter lane.

Attack the rim directly.

Occupy a corner.

Arrive ready to screen.

Or run ahead of the defense to establish interior position.

Do not always choose the same route.

If depth already exists, space the floor.

If presence near the rim is missing, create it.

The combination is especially useful when it recognizes early which space it needs to occupy.

---

# P09 · UNDERSTAND SCREENS

**Type:** BRIDGE

This intersection can use a screen from the perimeter and then become an interior presence, or begin as the screener and finish the action in another space.

Roll, cut, interior catch, pop, relocation, handoff or re-screen are ways to connect both functions.

The nuance lies in changing responsibility without breaking the possession.

---

# P10 · TRAIN THE BORING FUNDAMENTALS

**Type:** BRIDGE

This combination needs to connect perimeter and interior fundamentals.

Work especially on:

- oriented catches;
- open shooting;
- attacking closeouts;
- one or two functional dribbles;
- cuts;
- screening;
- rolling;
- pivots;
- ball protection;
- finishing with both hands;
- passing from interior positions;
- passing after help arrives;
- rebounding;
- defensive help;
- verticality near the rim.

Do not train these tools as two separate games.

Also work on their connections:

**pop → catch → attack;**

**cut → catch inside → finish or pass;**

**screen → roll → decide;**

**box out → run → occupy the next space.**

---

# P11 · LEARN BASKETBALL OFF THE COURT

**Type:** BRIDGE

Watch how perimeter and interior play connect within the same possession.

Notice when the player spaces, cuts, screens, receives inside, crashes the glass or helps near the rim, and how they return afterwards to a useful position.

---

# P21 · MAKE IT EASY TO PLAY WITH YOU

**Type:** BRIDGE

This combination can solve many needs within a possession without demanding a specific action.

Space the floor when someone else drives.

Cut when the defense loses a reference.

Screen when you can free someone.

Roll and force the defense to react.

Pass when help arrives.

Box out.

Protect the rim when the action places you there.

Then return to a useful space.

Versatility is there to complete the possession.

Not to constantly demonstrate how many functions you can perform.

---

# P22 · TALK

**Type:** BRIDGE

This combination can see the defense from very different perspectives.

From outside, you can detect cuts, screens and weak-side movement.

From interior positions, you can see drives, help, rebounding and threats near the rim.

Use both perspectives.

Communicate:

- screens;
- switches;
- help;
- recoveries;
- cuts;
- rebounding responsibilities;
- interior threats.

Changing areas also changes the information you can provide.

---

# P26 · YOUR DEVELOPMENT PLAN

**Type:** BRIDGE

The hybrid emphasis is connecting perimeter versatility with responsibilities near the rim.

Expansion should allow you to change space, screen, pass, rebound and help with interior protection without losing clarity about the dominant role.

---

# P27 · THE STATS THAT DO NOT SHOW UP

**Type:** BRIDGE

In this combination, also notice:

- moving from the perimeter to an interior threat through a cut or roll;
- boxing out from different locations;
- helping protect the rim and recovering afterwards;
- returning to a useful perimeter space after an interior action.

Ask yourself whether you changed areas without losing responsibility.
