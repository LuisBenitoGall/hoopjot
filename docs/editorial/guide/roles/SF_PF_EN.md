# BRIDGE PURPOSE

The intersection between small forward and power forward appears when a player can connect actions from perimeter, intermediate and interior spaces.

They can space the floor.

They can cut.

They can attack a closeout.

They can set a screen.

They can roll or pop afterwards.

They can help on the boards.

They can receive in intermediate spaces and move the ball.

They can defend different actions depending on what the possession needs.

This bridge does not turn both positions into a 50/50 identity.

The primary role pack always keeps the dominant perspective.

Only the nuances that come from changing space and responsibility without losing clarity are added here.

---

# P03 · YOUR FIRST MAJOR GOAL: BE RELIABLE

**Type:** BRIDGE

In this intersection, being reliable means contributing even when the possession places you in different functions.

You can maintain spacing.

Cut.

Screen.

Roll.

Attack a defensive recovery.

Help on the boards.

Help and recover.

The combination gains value when you can change responsibility without the team having to change its entire structure to accommodate you.

---

# P04 · DO NOT TRY TO LEARN EVERYTHING AT ONCE

**Type:** BRIDGE

You do not need to develop every perimeter tool of a small forward and every interior solution of a power forward at the same time.

First build a common base:

- occupy space well;
- catch ready;
- make open shots;
- cut with intention;
- attack clear closeouts;
- set legal screens;
- roll afterwards;
- make simple passes;
- box out;
- defend with discipline.

Then expand the situations from which you can contribute.

Versatility begins by making several basic responsibilities reliable.

Not by accumulating moves.

---

# P07 · REBOUNDING IS A DECISION

**Type:** BRIDGE

This combination can take part in rebounding from very different positions.

Sometimes you will be near the rim.

Other times you will begin on the perimeter.

The responsibility changes with your location, but the principle does not.

Locate and box out first.

Then attack the ball when appropriate.

From outside, pay attention to long rebounds and weak-side crashes.

From more interior positions, preventing your opponent from reaching the ball can be especially important even if someone else eventually secures it.

On offense, always distinguish between a good offensive rebounding opportunity and the need to protect transition balance.

---

# P08 · RUN

**Type:** BRIDGE

In transition you can help by creating both depth and width.

You can run to the rim.

Run a perimeter lane.

Arrive as the trailer.

Occupy a corner.

Arrive ready to screen.

Or attack a defense that is still recovering its positions.

Do not always choose the same path.

Read where the space is and which responsibility still needs to be filled.

The combination gains value when it completes the structure of transition instead of simply running to its usual area.

---

# P09 · UNDERSTAND SCREENS

**Type:** BRIDGE

This intersection can switch sides in screening actions quite naturally.

It can use a screen and, later in the same possession, become the screener to roll, pop, short roll, relocate or create a second action.

The combination gains value when that change of responsibility keeps the advantage alive.

---

# P10 · TRAIN THE BORING FUNDAMENTALS

**Type:** BRIDGE

This intersection needs fundamentals that work in different spaces.

Work especially on:

- oriented catches;
- open shooting;
- attacking closeouts;
- one or two functional dribbles;
- passing after penetration;
- cuts;
- screen positioning;
- roll and pop;
- catches in intermediate spaces;
- pivots;
- finishing;
- rebounding;
- defense on and off the ball.

Do not train “perimeter play” and “interior play” as unrelated compartments.

Also work on the transitions:

**pop → catch → attack;**

**screen → roll → decide;**

**cut → do not receive → occupy space again;**

**rebound → run → join the next action.**

---

# P11 · LEARN BASKETBALL OFF THE COURT

**Type:** BRIDGE

Watch how responsibility changes according to the possession.

Notice the relationship among spacing, cutting, screening, closeout attacks, rebounding and defensive help, especially after a first action that does not end with the player.

---

# P21 · MAKE IT EASY TO PLAY WITH YOU

**Type:** BRIDGE

This combination can simplify a possession because it can cover different responsibilities without demanding a special action.

Maintain space when needed.

Cut when an opportunity appears.

Screen to free someone else.

Roll or pop afterwards.

Attack a disorganized recovery.

Make the extra pass.

Box out.

Help and recover.

Versatility has value when it allows the possession to continue.

Not when it forces the team to constantly demonstrate all the things you can do.

---

# P26 · YOUR DEVELOPMENT PLAN

**Type:** BRIDGE

Development in this combination should increase the ability to change space and responsibility without losing reliability.

Useful versatility appears when perimeter play, screening, passing, rebounding and interior actions connect without erasing the main strengths.

---

# P27 · THE STATS THAT DO NOT SHOW UP

**Type:** BRIDGE

In this combination, notice especially:

- switching among spacing, cutting and screening without losing activity;
- attacking a closeout and preserving the advantage;
- helping on the boards from different positions;
- moving from a first action to another useful responsibility.

Ask yourself whether your versatility made the team's game simpler.
