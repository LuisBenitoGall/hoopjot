# ROLE PURPOSE

The power forward should provide physical presence without being limited to playing near the rim.

The role often connects interior and perimeter play.

They can screen.

They can roll.

They can pop.

They can receive on the short roll.

They can play from the high post.

They can attack a closeout.

They can rebound.

They can help near the rim.

They can pass from interior spaces.

Key priorities include:

- screening;
- roll and pop;
- short roll;
- high-post play;
- rebounding;
- second-chance opportunities;
- finishing;
- attacking closeouts;
- open shooting when appropriate;
- interior passing;
- help defense;
- secondary rim protection;
- communication.

A good power forward does not need to live permanently in the paint.

They need to understand which space to occupy so the team has better options.

This role pack intervenes only in the zones authorized by `GUIDE_ROLE_MATRIX_EN.md`.

# P01 · YOUR STARTING POINT

**Type:** INSERT

For a power forward, the first responsibilities are often tied to actions that help the team immediately.

Screen well.

Run.

Occupy useful spaces.

Box out.

Attack the ball.

Finish simple advantages.

Pass when help arrives.

Defend near and away from the rim according to the action.

You do not need to prove immediately that you have a complete set of interior moves or perimeter shooting.

You need to make the actions that can change the most possessions from your role reliable.

---

# P03 · YOUR FIRST MAJOR GOAL: BE RELIABLE

**Type:** INSERT

A reliable power forward gives stability to many possessions.

You can build that trust if you:

- set screens with good angles;
- roll or pop with intention;
- occupy the dunker spot, high post or perimeter spaces correctly when appropriate;
- box out;
- attack offensive rebounds without destroying team balance;
- finish simple advantages;
- make the right pass from interior positions;
- attack closeouts without complicating the action;
- provide defensive help;
- protect the rim when the situation requires it;
- communicate coverages and rebounding responsibilities.

The coaching staff should be able to trust that your presence improves the structure of the possession even when you do not receive the ball.

---

# P04 · DO NOT TRY TO LEARN EVERYTHING AT ONCE

**Type:** OVERRIDE

## LEVEL 1 · BE USEFUL

First build a reliable interior presence.

You should be able to:

- run in transition;
- occupy interior and roll spaces well;
- set legal screens;
- box out;
- attack rebounds;
- finish near the rim;
- catch and protect the ball;
- make simple passes;
- defend in help situations;
- communicate;
- occupy the right space when someone else drives.

You do not need to master many forms of creation yet.

You need to be useful in actions that repeat constantly.

## LEVEL 2 · CONTRIBUTE WITH CONTROL

Then add tools that allow you to use advantages.

Work especially on:

- rolling after a screen;
- popping when the space and your game allow it;
- short roll;
- receiving at the high post;
- passing from interior positions;
- finishing through contact;
- attacking closeouts;
- one or two functional dribbles;
- open shooting;
- reading help defense;
- defending the pick-and-roll according to the team's coverage.

Start turning your catches into quick decisions.

## LEVEL 3 · CREATE OR EXTEND ADVANTAGES

Then expand your solutions.

Learn to:

- pass from the short roll;
- play from the high post;
- attack a defensive switch;
- punish space with your shot;
- create after a closeout;
- connect a screen with a second action;
- use handoffs;
- find cuts from interior positions;
- finish with more solutions;
- alternate roll and pop according to the defense;
- defend different actions near and away from the rim.

Your variety should increase the team's possible answers.

It should not pull you away from the responsibilities that already make you useful.

# P06 · LEARN TO PLAY WITH YOUR BODY

**Type:** INSERT

For a power forward, using the body well has a lot to do with creating and protecting space.

Learn to:

- establish a good base when screening;
- gain position before receiving;
- protect the ball with your body;
- maintain balance when finishing through contact;
- change direction after a screen;
- decelerate to receive on the short roll;
- attack a closeout without losing control;
- box out before looking for the ball;
- maintain verticality near the rim;
- defend changes of direction without using your hands unnecessarily.

You do not need a particular body type to play this position.

You need to learn which physical advantages you have and how to apply them legally.

---

# P07 · REBOUNDING IS A DECISION

**Type:** INSERT

For a power forward, rebounding is often an important responsibility.

Do not wait under the rim.

Start earlier.

When the shot goes up:

- locate your opponent;
- create contact or gain position;
- box them out;
- then attack the ball.

On defense, your work may allow someone else to secure the rebound.

That ends the possession too.

On offense, learn to distinguish a good offensive rebounding opportunity from a situation where you need to protect team balance.

Attack especially from the weak side and along clear rebounding paths.

Do not confuse activity with jumping after every ball.

Good rebounding begins with reading, position and responsibility.

---

# P08 · RUN

**Type:** INSERT

In transition, a power forward can create advantages in several ways.

You can:

- run directly to the rim;
- establish an interior position early;
- create depth;
- set an early screen;
- arrive as the trailer;
- pop if the system and your shooting threat allow it;
- catch on the move;
- attack the offensive glass from a good path.

Do not jog toward an undefined area.

Run toward a responsibility.

If you arrive as the trailer, arrive ready to act.

Screen.

Pass.

Shoot.

Attack.

When possession is lost, get back with urgency.

Protect the rim and interior space first when that is your responsibility.

Then recover matchups and communicate.

# P09 · UNDERSTAND SCREENS

**Type:** OVERRIDE

As a power forward, screening can be one of your most consistent ways to create advantages.

Pay special attention to the timing and angle of the action, but above all to what happens next.

You can:

- roll hard to the rim;
- make a short roll;
- pop;
- change the angle;
- re-screen;
- offer a handoff;
- gain position;
- occupy a space that opens the driving lane.

Read the defensive response too.

If two defenders attack the ball, a short catch and passing decision may appear.

If the defense switches, there may be a matchup advantage.

If it drops, perimeter space may appear.

If it recovers early, re-screening or moving the ball may be the best solution.

For a power forward, the most important decision often begins after setting the screen.

---

# P10 · TRAIN THE BORING FUNDAMENTALS

**Type:** OVERRIDE

Your priority fundamentals should allow you to be reliable both near the rim and in intermediate spaces.

Work especially on:

- screen positioning and angles;
- footwork;
- catches;
- pivots;
- ball protection;
- finishing with both hands;
- using the backboard;
- finishing through contact;
- short roll;
- passing from the high post;
- passing from interior positions;
- passing after help arrives;
- one or two functional dribbles;
- attacking closeouts;
- open shooting when it is part of your role;
- rebounding and box outs;
- defensive verticality;
- defensive movement;
- help and recoveries.

You do not need to become a perimeter player to expand your game.

You need to add tools that make your main actions harder to defend.

And do not abandon interior fundamentals because you are developing your shot.

Spacing the floor has more value when the defense also has to respect your screens, rolls and rebounding.

# P11 · LEARN BASKETBALL OFF THE COURT

**Type:** OVERRIDE

When you watch a power forward, do not look only at points or rebounds.

Ask yourself:

- What angle do they use when screening?
- When do they roll and when do they pop?
- What do they do on the short roll?
- Where do they position themselves when someone else drives?
- How do they use the high post?
- When do they cut from the weak side?
- How do they pass from interior positions?
- When do they attack a closeout?
- How many dribbles do they need?
- How do they pursue rebounds?
- Who do they box out before jumping?
- How do they participate in help defense?
- When do they protect the rim?
- How do they recover after helping?
- What do they do immediately after setting a screen?

Pay special attention to the decisions they make after the first contact or first catch.

A good power forward often contributes a great deal in those second actions.

---

# P21 · MAKE IT EASY TO PLAY WITH YOU

**Type:** INSERT

As a power forward, you can make the game much easier for others.

Set screens that actually create an advantage.

Occupy the right space afterwards.

Do not close a driving lane by remaining where you are no longer useful.

Provide a passing lane on the short roll.

Find the open teammate when help arrives.

Box out so someone else can secure the rebound.

Protect the rim when help is needed.

Make the extra pass if the advantage continues outside.

A useful power forward connects interior and perimeter play.

They do not need to finish every action they help create.

---

# P22 · TALK

**Type:** INSERT

A power forward often takes part in defensive actions where communication needs to arrive early.

Communicate especially:

- pick-and-roll screens;
- pick-and-roll coverages;
- switches;
- help;
- recoveries;
- interior cuts;
- players in the dunker spot;
- rebounding;
- transition matchups;
- rim-protection situations.

If you are involved in a coverage, do not wait until contact to talk.

Early communication lets the on-ball defender know what they can expect behind them.

And after helping, communicate the recovery too.

The first rotation does not end the defense.

# P26 · YOUR DEVELOPMENT PLAN

**Type:** OVERRIDE

## PHASE 1 · RELIABILITY

First build a power forward who can be trusted in the actions that support the team.

Priorities:

- run;
- occupy space well;
- set legal, useful screens;
- roll with intention;
- box out;
- attack rebounds;
- finish simple advantages;
- make basic passes;
- defend in help situations;
- protect the rim when appropriate;
- communicate.

The goal is for your presence to add structure, activity and security.

## PHASE 2 · IMPACT

Start turning those actions into advantages that are harder to defend.

Develop:

- better screening angles;
- roll and pop;
- short roll;
- passing from the high post;
- passing after help arrives;
- attacking closeouts;
- open shooting;
- finishing through contact;
- offensive rebounding;
- reading defensive switches;
- more precise help defense;
- better secondary rim protection.

The goal is for the team to be able to use you in several ways without losing your main responsibilities.

## PHASE 3 · EXPAND YOUR GAME

Add tools that connect interior and perimeter play.

You can expand:

- creation from the short roll;
- reading from the high post;
- handoffs;
- shooting with greater range;
- attacking more complex closeouts;
- passing from different angles;
- playing against defensive switches;
- more varied finishes;
- secondary creation;
- defense against a wider range of actions;
- the ability to alternate screening, rolling, popping and a second action.

Expansion should increase your functional versatility.

It should not turn you into a less effective version of another position.

# P27 · THE STATS THAT DO NOT SHOW UP

**Type:** OVERRIDE

To evaluate your game as a power forward, also notice actions such as:

- setting a screen that creates an advantage;
- changing the angle of a screen;
- making a short roll that forces a rotation;
- finding a pass from the high post;
- boxing out an opponent so someone else gets the rebound;
- keeping an offensive rebound alive;
- occupying a space that opens a driving lane;
- making a help rotation that protects the rim;
- recovering after helping;
- switching correctly on defense;
- running to the rim and creating depth;
- arriving as the trailer and maintaining spacing;
- moving the ball after drawing help;
- making a second action after the screen.

After the game, you can ask yourself:

- Did I set screens that truly created advantages?
- Did I read what to do after screening well?
- Did I box out before looking for the ball?
- Did I generate second-chance opportunities?
- Did I occupy interior and perimeter spaces well?
- Did I take good shots?
- Did I attack closeouts well?
- Did I pass when help arrived?
- Did I protect the rim when appropriate?
- Did I communicate coverages?
