# ROLE PURPOSE

The small forward should be able to add value in many different situations.

That does not mean doing everything all the time.

It means recognizing what each possession needs and adapting without losing clarity.

Key priorities include:

- spacing;
- cuts;
- open shooting;
- attacking closeouts;
- transition;
- perimeter rebounding;
- defending different profiles;
- help and recoveries;
- second actions;
- simple passing;
- off-ball activity;
- the ability to connect advantages.

A good small forward may not initiate the possession and can still change it several times.

This role pack intervenes only in the zones authorized by `GUIDE_ROLE_MATRIX_EN.md`.

# P01 · YOUR STARTING POINT

**Type:** INSERT

For a small forward, one of the first responsibilities is learning to contribute without needing a special action designed for you.

Occupy space well.

Run.

Cut when an opportunity appears.

Shoot when you are truly open.

Attack an unbalanced defense.

Make the simple pass.

Help on the boards.

Defend in more than one area of the court.

You do not need to prove immediately that you can create from anywhere.

You need to become a useful presence in many different possessions.

---

# P03 · YOUR FIRST MAJOR GOAL: BE RELIABLE

**Type:** INSERT

A reliable small forward connects parts of the game.

You can build that trust if you:

- maintain good spacing;
- cut when the defense loses its references;
- are ready to shoot;
- attack closeouts without stopping the advantage;
- make simple passes on time;
- run in transition;
- help finish the possession on the boards;
- defend both on and off the ball;
- help and recover with discipline;
- keep participating after your first action.

The coaching staff should be able to trust that you do not need the ball to influence the possession.

---

# P04 · DO NOT TRY TO LEARN EVERYTHING AT ONCE

**Type:** OVERRIDE

## LEVEL 1 · BE USEFUL

First build a simple base of versatility.

You should be able to:

- occupy wings, corners, cutting lanes and intermediate spaces correctly when appropriate;
- cut with intention;
- run in transition;
- catch ready;
- make open shots;
- make the simple pass;
- defend on and off the ball;
- help and recover;
- box out;
- stay active after passing.

You do not need to create an advantage from nothing.

You need to recognize and use the advantages that already exist.

## LEVEL 2 · CONTRIBUTE WITH CONTROL

Then learn to improve an advantage when it reaches you.

Work especially on:

- attacking closeouts;
- one or two functional dribbles;
- finishing with both hands;
- passing after penetration;
- shooting after relocation;
- backdoor cuts;
- catches on the move;
- using off-ball screens;
- rebounding from perimeter positions;
- defending different types of attackers.

Start turning a good catch into a second good decision.

## LEVEL 3 · CREATE OR EXTEND ADVANTAGES

Then expand your solutions.

Learn to:

- attack a second rotation;
- create off the dribble when the possession requires it;
- use the pick-and-roll as a secondary handler;
- punish defensive switches;
- pass from intermediate positions;
- create from a catch on the move;
- play after a handoff;
- create advantages through cuts and screens;
- finish against different defensive profiles;
- maintain defensive impact across different matchups.

Versatility is not about accumulating tools.

It is about having the right tool when the situation changes.

# P06 · LEARN TO PLAY WITH YOUR BODY

**Type:** INSERT

For a small forward, using the body well means adapting it to very different situations.

Learn to:

- catch in balance;
- protect the ball when attacking a closeout;
- change direction without losing control;
- use your body on cuts;
- absorb contact when finishing;
- gain position on a long rebound;
- defend players with different size and speed;
- maintain a stable defensive base;
- contain a drive without opening your stance too much;
- use your arms and body without committing unnecessary fouls.

Physical versatility does not depend on having a particular body type.

It depends on learning how to use the body you have across different tasks.

---

# P07 · REBOUNDING IS A DECISION

**Type:** INSERT

Perimeter rebounding can be an important part of a small forward's work.

On defense:

- box out your assignment first;
- then go to the ball;
- pay attention to long rebounds;
- help when an interior teammate is occupied boxing out a bigger threat.

Your perimeter position can give you a good path to the rebound.

Use it without abandoning the team's balance.

On offense, recognize when you can attack a long rebound or enter from the weak side and when you need to protect defensive transition.

A small forward who helps finish defensive possessions greatly increases their usefulness without needing a single play called for them.

---

# P08 · RUN

**Type:** INSERT

In transition, the small forward should read early where they can create depth and space.

You can:

- run a perimeter lane;
- attack the rim directly if it is open;
- occupy a corner to open the floor;
- catch on the run;
- continue an advantage with a pass;
- attack a closeout before the defense is organized;
- crash from the weak side when team balance allows it.

If you secure a defensive rebound and have a clear outlet, you can advance with a pass or, when safe, handle the first part of transition.

Do not turn every rebound into an obligation to dribble.

Transition rewards early decisions.

When possession is lost, get back with urgency.

Protect space.

Find a threat.

Communicate.

# P09 · UNDERSTAND SCREENS

**Type:** OVERRIDE

As a small forward, you can take part on both sides of screening actions.

When using an off-ball screen, set up your defender with changes of pace and direction.

Then read what the defense has conceded:

- if they trail, preserve the advantage;
- if they anticipate the exit, a cut may appear;
- if they switch, recognize the new matchup;
- if you catch with space, decide quickly whether to shoot, pass or attack.

When you are the screener, your job continues after contact.

You can:

- pop;
- cut;
- roll;
- change position;
- offer a second action.

You should also be able to participate in pick-and-rolls and handoffs when the team's structure requires it.

Do not memorize only which side of the screen belongs to you.

Understand what advantage the action is trying to create and what responsibility comes next.

# P10 · TRAIN THE BORING FUNDAMENTALS

**Type:** OVERRIDE

Your priority fundamentals should allow you to be useful in different areas without losing simplicity.

Work especially on:

- foot preparation;
- oriented catches;
- catch-and-shoot;
- shooting from wings and corners;
- attacking closeouts;
- one or two functional dribbles;
- finishing with both hands;
- stops;
- pivots;
- passing after penetration;
- the extra pass;
- enough ball handling to preserve an advantage;
- cuts;
- off-ball footwork;
- perimeter rebounding;
- defensive movement;
- screen navigation;
- switching onto different profiles.

You do not need all these tools to be exceptional at the same time.

You need to make sure no basic responsibility becomes a constant problem for the team.

Versatility begins with fundamentals that are reliable enough.

# P11 · LEARN BASKETBALL OFF THE COURT

**Type:** OVERRIDE

When you watch a small forward, do not look only at how much they score.

Ask yourself:

- Where do they position themselves when someone else has the ball?
- When do they maintain spacing and when do they cut?
- How do they react after passing?
- When do they attack a closeout?
- How many dribbles do they use to decide?
- How do they contribute in transition?
- When do they crash from the perimeter?
- How do they use off-ball screens?
- When do they screen for another player?
- How do they react to a defensive switch?
- What do they do when they catch in an intermediate position?
- How do they help from the weak side?
- What types of attackers do they defend?
- How does their defense change with the matchup?
- What second action do they make after the first?

Pay special attention to possessions where they do several useful things without dominating any of them.

That is often where the real value of a good small forward appears.

# P21 · MAKE IT EASY TO PLAY WITH YOU

**Type:** INSERT

As a small forward, you can make the game easier by connecting actions.

Maintain space when a drive needs room.

Cut when the defense turns.

Make the extra pass.

Attack a disorganized recovery.

Screen if your movement can free someone else.

Help on the boards.

Run.

Defend where the team needs you.

After a first action, look for a second one.

Do not turn versatility into an excuse to try to solve everything.

Its value lies precisely in being able to do what the possession needs without breaking it.

---

# P22 · TALK

**Type:** INSERT

A small forward often occupies areas from which they can see both the ball and weak-side actions.

Communicate especially:

- cuts;
- off-ball screens;
- defensive switches;
- help;
- recoveries;
- shooters relocating;
- transition matchups;
- rebounding responsibilities;
- situations where a drive forces a rotation.

If you switch matchups or help on a drive, communicate what happens next.

Silent help can solve the first action and create a problem on the second.

# P26 · YOUR DEVELOPMENT PLAN

**Type:** OVERRIDE

## PHASE 1 · RELIABILITY

First build a small forward who can contribute without requiring the offense to adapt to their presence.

Priorities:

- maintain spacing;
- cut with good judgement;
- catch ready;
- make open shots;
- attack clear advantages;
- make simple passes;
- run in transition;
- box out;
- defend on and off the ball;
- help and recover;
- stay active after passing.

The goal is to contribute in different kinds of possessions without creating avoidable problems.

## PHASE 2 · IMPACT

Start turning your versatility into clearer advantages.

Develop:

- greater consistency on open shots;
- closeout attacks;
- finishing after one or two dribbles;
- passing after penetration;
- use of off-ball screens;
- better-timed cuts;
- perimeter rebounding;
- secondary creation;
- defense against different profiles;
- better help and recoveries;
- second actions after passing or screening.

The goal is to improve a possession from several areas of the court.

## PHASE 3 · EXPAND YOUR GAME

Add tools that increase your adaptability without erasing your main strengths.

You can expand:

- off-the-dribble creation;
- secondary pick-and-roll;
- play from intermediate positions;
- reading defensive switches;
- more varied finishes;
- passing after drawing help;
- handoff play;
- screening for teammates;
- shooting after movement;
- defense against a wider range of profiles;
- the ability to link several actions within the same possession.

Expansion should make you more adaptable.

Not more undefined.

Versatility does not mean doing everything.

It means being able to fulfil several roles without losing clarity about your main strengths.

# P27 · THE STATS THAT DO NOT SHOW UP

**Type:** OVERRIDE

To evaluate your game as a small forward, also notice actions such as:

- maintaining space that allows a drive;
- cutting at the right moment;
- making an extra pass;
- attacking a closeout without losing the advantage;
- helping finish a rebound;
- securing a long rebound;
- running a lane that forces the defense to open up;
- helping and recovering;
- defending different profiles correctly;
- setting a useful screen;
- using a screen without receiving the ball;
- making a second action after passing;
- occupying the weak side correctly;
- connecting two advantages with a simple decision.

After the game, you can ask yourself:

- Did I occupy space well?
- Did I cut when I should?
- Did I take good shots?
- Did I attack advantages well?
- Did I move the ball on time?
- Did I help on the boards?
- Did I defend different situations with discipline?
- Did I help and recover well?
- Did I keep participating after my first action?
- Did I make the game easier for the team?
